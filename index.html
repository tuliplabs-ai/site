From idea to production

A pipeline, not a leap of faith.

01Author

Describe the job in conversation — the agent, its policy, and its playbook are drafted together. Or write the definition by hand.

02Certify

The draft is exercised at escalating levels of trust — recorded fixtures, mock services, your staging systems, real endpoints on test credentials. The evidence is signed and pinned to the exact version.

03Publish

A named approver reviews the evidence and publishes an immutable version. An uncertified version cannot ship.

04Run

In your environment, next to your models and your data. Tulip's hosted services never see a credential or a customer record.

05Approve

The actions you flagged wait for a named person, who decides with everything in front of them — the action, the arguments, the policy line, the evidence.

06Audit

"What did this agent do in Q3, and who authorized it" is a query, not a project — exported straight into the systems your security team already watches.

How the platform works →

One action, end to end

The $2,000 refund

The whole product, in one moment.

Your support agent decides to issue a $2,000 refund. Your rules hold anything over $500 — so the run parks, and the hold lands in the queue with everything a person needs to decide. Someone with the authority approves, with a written justification. Only then is the payment credential injected — the model never saw it — and the action executes. Every step joins a record that fails verification if anyone edits it later.

What success looks like

A mid-market consumer-finance company had a support-agent prototype stuck in security review for a quarter. On Tulip, the team wrapped the same agent, wrote a twelve-line policy — refunds under $200 pass, anything above holds, chargebacks always hold, account closure is denied outright — and ran certification against their staging stack and their payment provider's test mode. The risk committee was shown the evidence bundle and the hash-chained record, and approved a production pilot in the company's own AWS account. Six weeks in: the agent handles the majority of routine refunds end to end, every hold has been decided by a named person with a recorded justification, and the audit export became part of the company's SOC 2 evidence instead of a new liability.

The lab

Clusiana · control models

Small models, built for control

Clusiana is our family of small control models — one for each decision an agent makes, trained to advise the gate and specialized from your own record. The first, a model that decides whether an action should run, is trained and tested; a grounding judge already ships in the runtime; run judgment and routing are next. None of them holds authority — a Clusiana opinion can never override your rules.

# drop the gate around an action your agent already takes
from tulip.control import (
    Action, admit, ControlPolicy, AuditTrail, AdmissionError,
)

policy = ControlPolicy()    # prod → human
trail  = AuditTrail()

risky = Action(name="refund", asset="cust:4821",
               blast_radius=1, kind="payment",
               environment="production")

try:
    await admit(risky, lambda: refund("cust:4821"),
                policy=policy, trail=trail)
except AdmissionError as e:
    print(e.decision.outcome)
    # → "require_human" — the refund did NOT run

About

Tulip Labs builds the control layer for AI agents that act.

The research is not the marketing arm of the product — it is where the product's claims get attacked in public. A runtime that says "the model cannot talk its way past the gate" and "the record cannot be forged" makes falsifiable claims, and falsifiable claims need a lab the way a lock company needs lockpickers. We publish the methods, merge the fixes upstream, disclose in coordination, and hold our own work to the bar we ask of an agent: state the scope, show the evidence, ship something a defender can run.

If our work touches a system you operate — or you'd like to collaborate — get in touch.

How we work

01Open by default

The work that matters is the work others can build on. The runtime is Apache-2.0; the research is published in full, tools included.

02Measured claims

Numbers with methods behind them. Before we claim something works, a test has to prove it against the real system — and a claim we can't prove is removed, not softened.

03Defender-first

Every security finding ships with something a defender can run today. We study attacks to build the audit, not the exploit.

04Reproducible

Experiments are numbered, scripted, and rerunnable. If you can't reproduce it, we haven't finished.
